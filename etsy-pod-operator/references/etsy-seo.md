# Etsy listing SEO

Three fields carry the ranking and the click: title, thirteen tags, description. Etsy
weights title and tags for retrieval, and the thumbnail plus title for the click.

## Title

**140 characters maximum.** Comma-separated keyword phrases, highest buyer-intent first,
because Etsy weights the front of the title and search results truncate the tail.

```
<exact target phrase>, <close variant>, <style or aesthetic>, <occasion or recipient>, Comfort Colors®
```

Worked example:

> Werewolf Ripping Shirt, Werewolf Bursting Through T-Shirt, Vintage Halloween Horror Graphic Tee, 90s Style Wolf Shirt, Comfort Colors®

The lead phrase is the researched high-volume keyword, verbatim. What follows are phrases a
different buyer might actually type, not synonyms padded in for the algorithm. A human
skimming search results should read the first two phrases and know what the shirt is.

Naming the garment brand at the end is allowed as a material descriptor and buyers do search
it — "comfort colors shirt" is a real query. Use the ® once.

## Tags

**Thirteen tags, 20 characters maximum each.** The character cap is the rule people get
wrong, and it silently costs you the phrase you researched: `werewolf ripping shirt` is 22
characters. It has to live in the title, with `werewolf ripping` as the tag.

Count characters rather than eyeballing.

Cover distinct search intents rather than thirteen rewordings of one:

- the exact target phrase, trimmed to fit
- two or three long-tail variants
- the aesthetic or style (`dark academia tee`, `90s graphic tshirt`, `vintage engraving`)
- the recipient (`gift for readers`, `wolf shirt men`, `gift for teens`)
- the occasion (`halloween shirt`, `spooky season gift`, `fall costume idea`)
- the garment (`comfort colors tee`)

Lowercase, no punctuation. Avoid repeating the same word across more than two or three tags
— thirteen tags all containing "skeleton" cover one query, not thirteen.

## Description

Etsy indexes the description weakly, so write it for the human deciding whether to buy.
Structure that works:

```
<one or two line hook — the joke, the feeling, the moment>

<what the artwork actually is: subject, style, technique, and what the garment does for it>

FIT
- unisex relaxed boxy fit, size down if between sizes
- heavyweight 6.1 oz 100% ring-spun US cotton, pre-shrunk, garment-dyed
- sewn-in label, no side seams, double-needle stitching

PRINT AND CARE
- direct-to-garment printing with water-based inks, soft hand feel
- machine wash cold inside out, tumble dry low, no bleach, no dry clean

SHIPPING
- made to order, production 2-5 business days then standard shipping

GIFTING
- who it's for, in two or three concrete lines
```

The hook is the part that sells. "You said you were five minutes away. That was several
seasons ago." does more than any keyword density.

The fit and care blocks are boilerplate — keep them consistent across the shop so buyers who
own one shirt know what they're getting.

**Only promise what fulfillment can do.** Printify prints and ships. There are no gift notes,
no rush processing, no combined-shipping discounts, no personalisation unless the listing
actually has it enabled. Copy that offers these generates support tickets and refunds. It's
an easy mistake to make when writing warm-sounding gifting copy — check the last section
specifically.

Adjust the fit and care details if the blank isn't a Comfort Colors 1717.

## IP screening

Applies to art, title, tags and description alike.

Avoid: franchise and character names, film and TV titles, band names, song lyrics, quoted
dialogue, recognisable logos and trade dress, and the names of living people.

Fine: generic formats of a joke, public-domain art traditions (danse macabre, memento mori,
woodcut and engraving styles), common seasonal language, and your own original phrasing.

When a high-volume keyword turns out to be a franchise — a web cartoon, a film, a game — drop
it and record why in the run report. The volume is real but so is the takedown.

## Cannibalisation

If the shop already ranks for a phrase, don't build a near-duplicate listing for it. Two of
your own listings competing for one keyword split the click-through and the sales history
that drives ranking. Pick a different angle inside the niche instead.
